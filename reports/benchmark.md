# Serving Benchmark — fp32 vs INT8

Compares the serving stack running the exported fp32 ONNX graph against the INT8
dynamically-quantized graph produced by `asap-quantize`, under identical load.

| **run2** | `model_quantized.onnx` (INT8, 136 MB) | 2026-10-03 14:47:35Z | 2 m 05 s | [report](../benchmarks/runs/run2/run2.html) · [csv](../benchmarks/runs/run2/run2_sheet.csv) |

---

## Headline

| Measure | run1 — fp32 | run2 — INT8 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `/predict` throughput | 8.89 rps | **14.20 rps** | **1.60× faster** |
| Aggregate throughput | 9.80 rps | **15.71 rps** | **1.60× faster** |
| `/predict` uncontended (min) | 39 ms | **22.4 ms** | **1.74× faster** |
| `/predict` p95 | 10,000 ms | **5,200 ms** | **1.92× lower** |
| Graph size on disk | 541.0 MB | **136.0 MB** | **3.98× smaller** |
| Label agreement vs fp32 | — | 96.88% | **3.1% of labels change** |
| `test_f1` | 0.9504 | 0.9436 | **−0.0068** |

INT8 is faster, far smaller, and slightly less accurate. The 0.0068 F1 cost is an
accepted trade for 1.6× throughput and a quarter of the disk footprint — see
[Accuracy trade-off](#accuracy-trade-off).

---

## Method

Load is generated by [`benchmarks/locustfile.py`](../benchmarks/locustfile.py) against a
running `asap-api` instance. Request bodies are drawn from a fixed, seeded sample of 500
real reviews taken from the test split, so both runs replay the same text.

| Parameter | run1 | run2 |
|---|---|---|
| Users | 100 (ramped from 1) | 100 (ramped from 1) |
| Think time | `between(0.5, 2.0)` s | `between(0.5, 2.0)` s |
| Task weights | `/predict` 10 · `/` 1 | `/predict` 10 · `/` 1 |
| Host | `http://127.0.0.1:8000` | `http://127.0.0.1:8000` |
| `inference.model_file` | `model.onnx` | `model_quantized.onnx` |
| Execution provider | `CPUExecutionProvider` | `CPUExecutionProvider` |
| `intra_op_num_threads` | 4 | 4 |
| `max_concurrent_inference` | 3 | 3 |

Hardware: 12 physical / 20 logical cores. Model: BERT, 12 layers, hidden size 768,
vocab 64,000, `max_length` 128.

Reproduce:

```bash
# fp32
ASAP_INFERENCE__MODEL_FILE=model.onnx asap-api
locust -f benchmarks/locustfile.py

# INT8 — build the graph first
asap-quantize
ASAP_INFERENCE__MODEL_FILE=model_quantized.onnx asap-api
locust -f benchmarks/locustfile.py
```

## Analysis

### INT8 lifts the throughput ceiling by 1.6×

`/predict` moved from 8.89 to 14.20 rps. This is the first change in the serving
programme to move throughput at all: a sweep of `max_concurrent_inference ×
intra_op_num_threads` over `1×4`, `3×4`, `6×2` and `12×1` held near 10 rps for every
pairing, within run-to-run noise.

The `min` column isolates the model from the queue. An uncontended `/predict` fell from
**39 ms to 22.4 ms**, a 1.74× reduction in actual service time. Since roughly 99.6% of a
request is `session.run` — `normalize` 0.05 ms, `tokenize` 0.23 ms — a faster graph is
close to the only thing that can move the ceiling, and the measurement agrees.

### Both runs are saturated, so medians are queue depth

100 users with ~1.25 s of think time can offer at most ~80 rps. Against 8.89 rps (fp32)
that is ~9× oversubscription; against 14.20 rps (INT8) it is ~6×. In both cases the
latency columns describe the queue, not the model.

Little's Law confirms run1 reached steady state: `100 ÷ 9.80 − 1.25 ≈ 8.9 s`, against a
measured median of 9.3 s.

Run2 did **not** fully settle. The same arithmetic predicts `100 ÷ 15.71 − 1.25 ≈ 5.1 s`,
which matches run2's **p95 of 5.2 s** rather than its 2.5 s median. Run2 lasted 2 m 05 s,
so the ramp occupies a far larger share of the run and pulls the median down. The
honest steady-state comparison is therefore **9.3 s (fp32) against ~5.2 s (INT8)**, a 1.8×
reduction — not the 3.4× a naive median-to-median reading suggests.

### The 1.6× throughput figure is conservative

Throughput is averaged over the whole run, including the ramp, when fewer users are
generating load. Run2 spends proportionally more of its life ramping, which depresses its
average rps. The true steady-state advantage is therefore at least 1.6×.

### The health endpoint stays responsive in both runs

`GET /` held a 5–6 ms median in both runs while `/predict` sat in the seconds. Health
checks do not acquire an inference slot, so readiness probes and load-balancer checks
remain trustworthy even when the service is deeply saturated.

## Accuracy trade-off

The speed is not free. Measured by `asap-quantize --check`:

| Criterion | Measured | Design's provisional threshold |
|---|---:|---:|
| `test_f1` | 0.9504 → **0.9436** (−0.0068) | ≤ 0.005 |
| Label agreement vs fp32 | 0.9688 | ≥ 0.99 |
| Max absolute logit delta | 3.2179 | — expected for INT8 |

INT8 costs **0.68 F1 points** and changes **3.1% of predictions** — roughly one review
in 32 receives a different label.

**This cost is accepted.** The thresholds in the
[serving performance design](../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-26-serving-performance-design.md)
were written before any of this was measured, as a provisional budget rather than a
product requirement. Weighed against 1.6× throughput, a 1.74× faster uncontended
response and a 3.98× smaller graph, a 0.0068 F1 movement on a binary sentiment
classifier is a reasonable price. `model_quantized.onnx` is the served graph.

Two notes for anyone revisiting this:

- The judgement is workload-specific. A use case where a 3% label change is material —
  automated moderation, anything with a downstream threshold on confidence — should
  re-weigh it rather than inherit this decision.
- **Static quantization with a calibration set** typically recovers most of what dynamic
  quantization gives up, at the same speed. It is the way to stop paying this cost at
  all, and remains worthwhile even though the current trade is accepted.

## Limitations

- **Single run per configuration.** Neither figure is replicated. Repeat runs of identical
  configurations on this hardware have varied by up to 5×. A median of three with the
  observed spread would be a sounder basis for a decision.
- **Unequal durations.** run1 ran 6 m 47 s and run2 2 m 05 s. Run2 did not reach steady
  state, so its median is not comparable to run1's; the p95 figures are.
- **Latency here is not service latency.** At 6–9× oversubscription no median or
  percentile describes the model. The `min` column is the closest available proxy, and
  measuring service latency properly requires staying below saturation.
- **`GET /` percentiles are undersampled** — 189 requests in run2 cannot support a p99.
- **The F1 comparison is on a 5,000-row seeded subsample** of the 18,533-row test split,
  with both graphs scored on the same rows. A full fp32 pass exceeds 30 minutes on this
  hardware. The paired delta is sound; the absolute values are not full-split numbers.
- **Server configuration is recorded by hand.** The harness does not read settings back
  from the live server.

---

## Conclusions

1. **INT8 dynamic quantization is the first change to move the throughput ceiling**, from
   8.89 to 14.20 rps on `/predict`, with uncontended service time down from 39 ms to
   22.4 ms and the graph 3.98× smaller on disk.
2. **It costs 0.68 F1 points** and changes 3.1% of labels — above the design's
   provisional 0.005 budget, and **accepted** as the price of the speed and size gains.
   INT8 is the served graph.
3. The ceiling is set by model cost, not request concurrency. Roughly 99.6% of a request
   is `session.run`, and no limiter or thread-count pairing moved throughput; a cheaper
   graph did, on the first attempt.
4. **Static quantization with a calibration set** would likely keep the speed while
   recovering most of the accuracy, and is worth pursuing even with the current trade
   accepted.
